In a previous study, [Julia Davis-Paroda et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.immuni.2024.10.003) presented and analyzed data from a New York organ donor tissue database to explore tissue-resident immune cells specific to SARS-CoV-2 in vaccinated and previously infected organ donors. Here we re-analyze this dataset and additional samples with various statistical and machine learning (ML) methods to investigate the relation between on the one hand SARS-CoV-2-specific T and B cell subset frequencies across tissues and in blood, and on the other hand a panel of SARS-CoV-2-specific antibody titers. We show that tissue-resident cells may be more predictive of high titers than blood cell percentages, and develop a structural equation model with imposed causal relations that makes immunological sense and is compatible with the data. One interesting statistical / data science aspect is the fact that we had to rely on multiple imputation (using the MICE R package), as the tissue sample data had many missing observations, making direct application of many of-the-shelf ML methods (RF, elastic net, CCA) infeasible.
